The French Revolution began in 1789 after years of social inequality, financial crisis, and political conflict in France. The estates system gave enormous privilege to the clergy and nobility while the Third Estate carried the burden of taxes and had little political power. Rising bread prices and debt from wars made life harder for ordinary people. The storming of the Bastille became a symbol of resistance, and the National Assembly emerged as the Third Estate pushed for reform. The Revolution introduced major changes including the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, the abolition of feudal privileges, and the execution of King Louis XVI. It also led to the Reign of Terror, showing how revolutionary ideals could be enforced through violence and fear.
